Death of the Considered Opinion

An opinion used to take a week. You read the thing, you slept on it, you talked to one or two people whose taste you trusted, you found yourself revising on the walk home. The opinion that emerged was small, but it was yours, and it could withstand a follow-up question.

An opinion now takes ninety seconds. It arrives fully-formed because it was never formed at all — it was selected from a menu of plausible reactions and ratified by a small applause of likes. Follow-up questions feel hostile because there is no foundation under the answer for the question to land on.

I am writing this on a platform that rewards me for not writing it carefully. I am aware. I am doing it anyway. That, too, is a kind of considered opinion.

Yours, still logged in — The Editor
