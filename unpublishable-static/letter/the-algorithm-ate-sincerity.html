The Algorithm Ate Sincerity

Earnestness used to be a posture you could fall out of. You wrote a thing, you meant it, the next day you weren't sure, you walked it back at dinner. Now the post is the dinner; the walk-back is a quote-tweet you'll add to the regret folder. There is no recovery from the algorithmic record of your own face being right.

What the feed has done — and this is the part nobody on the feed is allowed to say — is laminate sincerity. It made the soft thing hard. It put a frame around it and a counter underneath it. The friend who used to confide in you now confides at you, in front of a camera, on behalf of an audience that includes you only incidentally.

I am not nostalgic for a pre-internet honesty. There was no such honesty. There was provincialism and small social punishment, which is a different kind of fakeness. What I miss is the ability to test an opinion in low stakes. To half-mean something. To be working it out.

The platforms know this. They have decided that working-it-out is bad content. Working-it-out has no thumbnail.

Yours, still logged in — The Editor
